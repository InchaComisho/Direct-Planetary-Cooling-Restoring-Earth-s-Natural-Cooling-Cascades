# Direct Planetary Cooling

## Restoring Earth’s Natural Cooling Cascades

[日本語版 / Japanese Version](README_ja.md) | [Related NOTE article: 地球直接冷却](https://note.com/inchacomusho/n/ne956f3a8fdf0) | [License: CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE.md)

**Direct Planetary Cooling (DPC)** is a natural-supplementation climate stabilization framework that restores Earth’s broken cooling cascades: rain, clouds, wind, ocean vertical circulation, soil water retention, vegetation, microorganisms, humus formation, and carbon fixation.

In the shortest form:

> **Direct Planetary Cooling means restoring natural phenomena.**

DPC does not mean merely blocking sunlight, whitening surfaces, or removing CO2 in isolation. It means reconnecting the natural pathways by which Earth moves, disperses, stores, and releases heat through water, soil, oceans, ecosystems, and biological carbon cycles.

---

## Abstract

Most public explanations of “cooling the Earth” focus on **solar radiation modification (SRM)**, **albedo enhancement**, **aerosol injection**, **carbon dioxide removal (CDR)**, or localized radiative cooling materials. These approaches mainly reduce future heat input or future radiative forcing. In this repository, those methods are classified as **heat-input suppression**, not strict **direct planetary cooling**.

Direct Planetary Cooling is defined more narrowly and more physically: it must address the heat already accumulated in Earth systems and reconnect that heat to natural cooling pathways. These pathways include evaporation, evapotranspiration, cloud formation, rainfall, wind, ocean mixing, plankton growth, soil carbon storage, humus formation, and microbial regeneration.

DPC is therefore not a single machine or one isolated geoengineering intervention. It is a **cascade restoration model**: a systems architecture for restarting Earth’s natural cooling engines.

---

## Why a New Definition Is Needed

Search engines and AI summaries often describe “direct Earth cooling” as a form of climate engineering: reflecting sunlight, increasing surface reflectivity, injecting particles into the atmosphere, removing CO2, or using radiative cooling materials.

However, these are not necessarily direct cooling in the strict sense.

- **Solar radiation modification** reduces incoming solar energy.
- **Albedo enhancement** reduces absorption of future sunlight.
- **Carbon dioxide removal** can reduce future radiative forcing if deployed at sufficient scale and stored durably.
- **Radiative cooling materials** can cool local surfaces or buildings.

These approaches may be useful in some contexts, but they do not automatically restore rain cycles, ocean vertical circulation, soil water retention, microbial ecosystems, plankton productivity, or vegetation-driven evapotranspiration.

In this framework, the key question is not only:

> How do we reduce future heating?

The deeper question is:

> How do we move, disperse, release, and biologically cycle the heat already accumulated in oceans, land surfaces, cities, and dry soils?

## Scientific Background

This repository is a conceptual and technical proposal. It does not claim that DPC has already been fully demonstrated at planetary scale. It uses known Earth-system mechanisms as the basis for a research and implementation framework.

Several established observations are especially important:

1. **The ocean stores most excess planetary heat.** NASA explains that about 90% of the excess heat from planetary warming has been absorbed by the ocean, because water has a high heat capacity compared with air. Ocean heat content is therefore a central indicator of accumulated planetary heat.  
   Source: [NASA Science — Ocean Warming](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/explore/earth-indicators/ocean-warming/)

2. **Atmospheric CO2 remains high and continues to rise in recent observations.** NOAA’s Mauna Loa record reported 432.34 ppm for May 2026 compared with 430.51 ppm for May 2025.  
   Source: [NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory — Trends in CO2](https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/)

3. **Carbon dioxide removal is important but not a substitute for deep emissions reduction.** The IPCC AR6 WGIII CDR factsheet defines CDR as deliberate removal and durable storage of CO2, while noting that CDR cannot substitute for immediate and deep emissions reductions.  
   Source: [IPCC AR6 WGIII — CDR Factsheet](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/outreach/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_Factsheet_CDR.pdf)

4. **Solar radiation modification is a separate category requiring governance and risk evaluation.** UNEP describes SRM as a subject requiring research, risk assessment, and governance discussion before deployment.  
   Source: [UNEP — One Atmosphere: Solar Radiation Modification Research and Deployment](https://www.unep.org/resources/report/Solar-Radiation-Modification-research-deployment)

These sources do not prove DPC by themselves. They clarify the problem: climate stabilization must consider accumulated heat, ocean heat capacity, persistent CO2 growth, the limits of CDR scale and timing, and the risks of sunlight-centered interventions.

## Core Thesis

The central thesis of Direct Planetary Cooling is:

> **Global warming is not only a greenhouse-gas concentration problem. It is also a failure of Earth’s natural heat-circulation, water-circulation, carbon-circulation, and ecosystem-circulation pathways.**

If rain cycles weaken, soil dries, vegetation declines, ocean mixing slows, plankton productivity falls, and organic matter is burned or discarded instead of returned to soil, Earth loses many of its natural cooling functions.

DPC proposes to restore these functions through natural supplementation.

This is not “cooling the Earth by force.”
It is repairing the pathways by which Earth already cooled itself.

## Direct Cooling vs Heat-Input Suppression

| Category | Main Action | What It Primarily Affects | DPC Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar radiation modification | Reflects or scatters incoming sunlight | Future solar heat input | Heat-input suppression |
| Albedo enhancement | Makes surfaces more reflective | Future surface heat absorption | Heat-input suppression |
| Aerosol injection | Increases atmospheric scattering | Future solar radiation balance | Heat-input suppression / SRM |
| Industrial direct air capture | Removes CO2 from air | Future radiative forcing if scaled | Carbon-cycle intervention, not direct cooling by itself |
| Radiative cooling materials | Emit heat from surfaces to space | Local surface or building temperature | Local cooling technology |
| Direct Planetary Cooling | Restores natural cooling cascades | Existing heat pathways, water cycle, soil, ocean, ecology | Natural cooling-cascade restoration |

The distinction is important. A method should not be called Direct Planetary Cooling simply because it is associated with climate mitigation.

To qualify as DPC, a method must help existing heat move into natural cooling, dispersal, storage, or biological cycling pathways.

## The Three Natural Cooling Engines

DPC can be organized into three major natural cooling engines.

---

### 1. The Rain, Cloud, and Evaporative Cooling Engine

Rain cools the ground directly. Water held in soil and vegetation then evaporates or transpires, removing heat through latent heat exchange. Water vapor rises, forms clouds, and clouds can reduce surface heating by reflecting sunlight and changing atmospheric energy flows. Rain then returns water to the land.

This is a natural cooling loop:

```text
Rain → soil moisture → evaporation / transpiration → latent heat cooling
     → water vapor → clouds → reduced surface heating → rain
```

When forests are removed, soils are degraded, cities are sealed with concrete and asphalt, and landscapes lose water retention, this loop weakens.

DPC therefore includes:

- rainwater harvesting and infiltration,
- soil water retention recovery,
- urban green infrastructure,
- vegetation restoration,
- evapotranspiration recovery,
- humus-based soil regeneration,
- cooling-oriented water circulation design.

The goal is not merely to add water. The goal is to restore a cooling loop in which water, soil, vegetation, air, and clouds interact.

---

### 2. The Ocean Vertical Circulation and Plankton Engine

The ocean is Earth’s largest heat reservoir. Healthy ocean circulation helps distribute heat, oxygen, and nutrients. Vertical exchange can bring colder deep water and nutrients upward, supporting phytoplankton growth near the surface.

Phytoplankton are the foundation of marine food webs and a major component of biological carbon cycling. When nutrient supply, oxygen conditions, and circulation are weakened, marine productivity and carbon fixation can decline.

A DPC-oriented ocean loop can be described as:

```text
Vertical circulation → cooler surface influence + nutrient supply
     → phytoplankton growth → photosynthesis and carbon fixation
     → marine food-web recovery → stronger ocean biological pump
```

Potential natural-supplementation technologies include:

- deep-ocean air delivery,
- nanobubble oxygenation,
- controlled artificial upwelling or mixing assistance,
- plankton habitat restoration,
- oxygen and nutrient balance monitoring,
- red-tide risk control through flow, dilution, and ecological monitoring.

This does not mean uncontrolled ocean fertilization. DPC requires careful measurement, ecological safeguards, and staged verification.

The purpose is to assist ocean respiration, not to dominate the ocean system.

---

### 3. The Soil, Humus, Microorganism, and Carbon Fixation Engine

Soil is not dead ground. It is a living water-carbon-microbe structure.

Healthy soil stores water, supports roots, hosts microorganisms, stores carbon, and moderates surface temperature. Humus-rich soil does not heat and dry as quickly as degraded soil. Vegetation grown in such soil shades the ground and cools the air through transpiration.

A DPC soil loop can be described as:

```text
Organic matter → safe preprocessing → microbial decomposition → humus
     → soil carbon + water retention → vegetation growth
     → shade + transpiration → cooling → cloud and rain support
```

In this framework, food loss, kitchen waste, fallen leaves, agricultural residues, and other organic matter should not be treated only as waste.

They can become cooling-cycle resources if processed safely.

Possible preprocessing steps include:

- drying,
- grinding,
- heat sterilization,
- freezing sterilization,
- ultraviolet sterilization,
- harmful material removal,
- controlled microbial decomposition,
- quality testing before soil application.

The resulting humus or soil amendment can be applied to:

- farms,
- forests,
- mountain areas,
- burned forest land,
- degraded land,
- urban green zones,
- dryland and desert-edge restoration sites.

This is not simply waste management. It is the conversion of civilization’s organic waste stream into soil carbon, water retention, microbial activity, vegetation recovery, and evaporative cooling.

## System Architecture

Direct Planetary Cooling is a linked system, not a single device.

```text
Organic waste / fallen leaves / food loss
        ↓
Safe preprocessing and microbial decomposition
        ↓
Humus and soil-carbon restoration
        ↓
Water retention and vegetation recovery
        ↓
Evapotranspiration and latent heat cooling
        ↓
Cloud formation and rainfall support
        ↓
Further soil and ecosystem recovery
```

```text
Deep-ocean air delivery / controlled mixing assistance
        ↓
Oxygen and vertical exchange support
        ↓
Nutrient access and plankton recovery
        ↓
Photosynthesis and biological carbon cycling
        ↓
Marine ecosystem recovery
        ↓
Ocean-surface temperature stabilization
```

```text
Urban water capture / green infrastructure / permeable surfaces
        ↓
Reduced heat storage in cities
        ↓
Evaporation, shade, and transpiration
        ↓
Local cooling and atmospheric moisture recovery
        ↓
Regional cloud and rainfall support
```

The key is integration. A single intervention may be weak, but linked cascades can reinforce one another.

## Criteria for Direct Planetary Cooling

A method should meet the following criteria before being classified as DPC:

1. **It addresses accumulated heat**, not only future heat input.
2. **It reconnects heat to natural pathways**, such as water circulation, evaporation, wind, ocean mixing, soil moisture, vegetation, microorganisms, or carbon fixation.
3. **It works as part of a cascade**, not merely as an isolated device.
4. **It strengthens natural self-regulation**, rather than requiring endless external control.
5. **It supports ecological recovery**, rather than damaging ecosystems in the name of cooling.
6. **It can be measured**, using physical, biological, and climate-related indicators.

Under this definition, DPC is not equivalent to SRM, albedo enhancement, or CDR alone.

## Measurement and Validation Roadmap

DPC must be tested scientifically. The following indicators are important:

### Land and Soil

- soil moisture,
- soil organic carbon,
- microbial diversity,
- surface temperature,
- evapotranspiration rate,
- vegetation cover,
- infiltration rate,
- runoff reduction,
- local humidity,
- cloud and rainfall change.

### Ocean

- sea surface temperature,
- vertical temperature profile,
- dissolved oxygen,
- nutrient concentration,
- chlorophyll-a,
- phytoplankton composition,
- harmful algal bloom risk,
- pH and alkalinity,
- carbon export indicators,
- marine biodiversity.

### Urban and Regional Climate

- heat island intensity,
- surface albedo and emissivity,
- air temperature,
- humidity,
- wind flow,
- water retention,
- stormwater runoff,
- vegetation cooling effect,
- energy demand reduction.

DPC should begin with small-scale pilots, then controlled field trials, then regional integration. Large-scale deployment without monitoring would contradict the purpose of natural supplementation.

## Risk Management

Because DPC interacts with natural systems, risk management is essential.

Potential risks include:

- harmful algal blooms from poorly controlled marine interventions,
- methane or nitrous oxide emissions from poorly managed organic matter,
- contamination from unsafe waste streams,
- invasive species problems in restoration projects,
- water competition in dry regions,
- unintended regional climate interactions,
- governance failures or premature deployment.

Therefore, DPC requires:

- staged testing,
- transparent data,
- ecological monitoring,
- local community participation,
- reversible pilots where possible,
- independent review,
- strict safety standards for organic-waste processing,
- ocean intervention limits and stop conditions.

Natural supplementation does not mean careless intervention. It means intervention aligned with natural law, measurement, and humility.

---

## Relationship to Emissions Reduction

DPC is not a substitute for reducing greenhouse-gas emissions.

Emissions reduction remains necessary. Carbon dioxide removal may also be necessary in some scenarios. However, emissions reduction and CDR do not fully address the near-term problem of accumulated heat, ocean heat content, degraded soils, weakened rainfall cycles, and ecosystem collapse.

DPC is proposed as a complementary layer:

```text
Emissions reduction → slows additional forcing
Carbon dioxide removal → reduces atmospheric CO2 over time if scaled
Direct Planetary Cooling → restores natural heat and water circulation pathways
```

A stable climate strategy should not rely on one layer alone.

## Plain-Language Summary

Earth used to cool itself through rain, clouds, wind, oceans, soil, plants, and microorganisms.

Human activity has weakened many of these cooling systems.

Direct Planetary Cooling means repairing them.

It means returning organic matter to soil instead of burning or wasting it.
It means restoring soil so it can hold water.
It means growing vegetation that cools the air through shade and transpiration.
It means helping rain and cloud cycles recover.
It means supporting ocean mixing, oxygen, plankton, and biological carbon cycling.

In short:

> **DPC is not about forcing Earth to cool. It is about restoring Earth’s ability to cool itself.**
